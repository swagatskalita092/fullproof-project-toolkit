# Pick your idea

Before scoping anything, stress-test the idea itself: would hundreds of other people plausibly submit the identical pitch? A trendy shape ("wrap an LLM," "build a CRUD app with auth," "clone a famous app") isn't automatically disqualifying - a conventional problem built with real depth (real concurrency handling, real authorization boundaries, real failure analysis) is often stronger than a novel-sounding idea with shallow execution.

**The actual test:** name the differentiator before building, not after. Ask honestly - what does this prove that a shallow, generic version of the same idea wouldn't? If the honest answer is "nothing beyond gluing two tools together," the idea needs a sharper angle or a replacement, not more features.

**Getting a second opinion, done properly:** the value isn't a verdict to defer to, it's a source of objections.
1. Write your own criteria and your own initial assessment first.
2. Ask specifically for counterexamples, hidden assumptions, and the strongest reason to reject the idea - not "is this good," which invites a persuasive verdict instead of useful scrutiny.
3. Check any factual claims against real evidence rather than accepting them because they sound authoritative.
4. Record what changed the decision and why - if nothing changed, say that too.

**In practice:** write one sentence for what the project does, one sentence for its shallow common version, one sentence for what your version proves beyond that. If you're stuck without an idea at all, start from a real invariant ("what must never happen") in a domain you're genuinely curious about, rather than a feature list.
