# Research paper drafting

The same discipline that makes an engineering project credible applies directly to a research paper: name the real claim, prove it, document honestly, never fabricate a number.

**Before writing, have an actual result.** Run the experiment and get the real numbers first - a paper drafted before the result exists tends to subtly shape the experiment to match the narrative already being written.

**Structure, and why each part exists:** Abstract (the actual finding, not a teaser) -> Introduction (a falsifiable research question, not just a topic) -> Related work (papers actually read, not cited secondhand) -> Method (detailed enough for someone else to reproduce it) -> Results (the real numbers, including inconvenient ones, each with its method stated) -> Discussion/Limitations (what this does and doesn't show, stated plainly) -> Conclusion (what was actually found, not what was hoped for).

**The honesty rules, carried over directly:**
- No fabricated or rounded numbers - every figure traces to something actually measured, with its method stated.
- Don't overclaim from a small or convenient sample - state the real sample size and conditions next to every claim.
- Report negative or inconclusive results honestly rather than omitting runs that didn't support the hypothesis.
- Separate observation from speculation - clearly different language for "the data shows X" versus "this might suggest Y, though Z would be needed to confirm it."
- Disclose AI-tool use wherever required, rather than letting a convenient omission become a credibility problem later.

**In practice:** write the falsifiable question before running anything. Write Method first while the procedure is fresh. Write Results from the captured data, not memory. Write Discussion/Limitations honestly before writing the Abstract, so the abstract summarizes what the paper actually shows.
